# Uneton data inventory and public notices

The authoritative SQLite baseline is `platform/backend/internal/store/migrations/001_initial.sql`. `privacy-data-inventory.json` maps every table to the Apple data categories it can contain. `mise run privacy:check` compares the baseline digest and table set with that inventory, then checks the App Store declaration and `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`. A schema edit intentionally stops the check until the inventory and both declarations are reviewed. This is a review gate, not automatic interpretation of a new column's meaning.

The public policy and terms are the HTML templates in `platform/backend/internal/legal/`. The backend fills the operator and contact email from runtime configuration, then serves them at `/privacy`, `/terms`, and `/support` on `api.uneton.app`; the app links to the same pages. The personal values and App Review contact are age-encrypted in `fnox.toml` for local work, while production reads them from its private environment. When the behavior below changes, update the inventory, declarations, public pages, and effective dates together. App Store Connect changes are applied only through the local store commands after a remote plan.

| Data | Source and purpose | Retention and deletion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple subject, provided display name, encrypted refresh credential | `users`; sign-in, account recovery, credential audit and revocation | Kept while the account is active. Account erasure clears private identity and credential material and leaves a referential tombstone. |
| Family membership and invitations | `families`, `family_members`, `invites`; share the diary with chosen caregivers | Membership ends on caregiver removal, leaving the family, or account deletion. An owner may transfer ownership to another caregiver; deleting a family requires removing other caregivers first. A family also survives account deletion when another caregiver inherits ownership. Invitations can be revoked; expired database rows are not currently pruned. |
| Child settings, sleep history, growth and temperature observations | `children`, `sleep_sessions`, `growth_measurements`, `temperature_readings`; diary, reminders, forecasts, growth charts and temperature history | Kept with the shared family. A baby or diary item deleted in the app has a tombstone for sync; physical removal of its server records is not guaranteed until its family is deleted. This is health-related child data. Temperature readings are visible to invited caregivers and are not used for diagnosis or sleep prediction. |
| Device and notification identifiers | `devices`, `live_activity_tokens`, `live_activity_starts`; authentication, APNs, Live Activities, notification language and bounded remote-reminder ownership | Device sign-out or account deletion removes the device row and associated push tokens. Registration revision watermarks prevent delayed uploads from replacing newer tokens. Invalid tokens may be cleared earlier. |
| Commands, events, snapshots and delivery payloads | `commands`, `sync_events`, `family_sync_snapshots`, `deliveries`; idempotent sync, restore recovery and push delivery | Command results and snapshots may remain for the family's lifetime. Events compact behind a snapshot. Sent deliveries are pruned after seven days; pending delivery may remain until resolved. |
| Operational diagnostics | Backend panic/error logs and container runtime; reliability and security | The code does not intentionally log request bodies or credentials. The repository does not currently enforce a log retention period. Operators must configure host journal retention before production and review any new telemetry fields. |
| Backup replica | Litestream's copy of the authoritative SQLite database | The configured replica rotates snapshots and WAL on a 24-hour retention period. A deleted record may persist until that rotation. Restore requires reapplying post-snapshot deletion and rotating the sync generation. |
| Device-local records | SQLite projection, pending commands, acknowledged commands (kept for the server-advertised restore window, seven days by default, then deleted on the next sync), Keychain credentials, UserDefaults device and reminder preferences, and the paired Watch's latest presentation snapshot and one unanswered action (which may include a temperature note) | These support offline work and authentication. The Watch clears its saved action after a definitive phone reply; an unanswered action remains on the Watch until the phone can reply or the Watch app is removed. Sign-out clears account-scoped phone state only after pending changes have synchronized and publishes an empty Watch snapshot when WatchConnectivity is available; the Watch can retain an older presentation snapshot or unanswered action until that update and reply arrive or its app is removed. These are not a separate server-held dataset. |
| Static growth reference points | `growth_reference_points`; non-family chart reference | The reference is not personal information and is excluded from the App Store collection declaration. |

Family invitation links contain the existing bearer invitation token in an HTTPS path. The public landing page exposes no family or child information, never claims an invitation, uses no cookies or external resources, and sends no-store and no-referrer headers. Share links only with intended caregivers. The backend redacts invitation paths from panic logs; any proxy/access logging configured by operators must also avoid recording these tokens. This adds no stored fields, processors, collection categories, or retention changes.

Remote sleep reminders are derived from acknowledged sleep and child settings. `sleep_reminders` stores device, family, child and wake-session identifiers, target/due times, and whether an APNs submission was attempted. It does not store a separate diary. The worker removes records whose due time is more than seven days old; sign-out/account/device erasure and family deletion remove related rows through foreign-key cascades. Baby deletion cancels delivery while its sync tombstone remains. The phone stores a bounded ownership timestamp in app-only UserDefaults to prevent a local alert from duplicating a remotely owned reminder, including after a lost response or restart. Ownership is renewed for 24 hours when push registration and synchronization occur. Remote reminders use the existing notification switch and lead time, English or Finnish language, and APNs provider. They add no collection category, processor, or age-rating answer; the existing linked DEVICE_ID, HEALTH, and OTHER_USAGE_DATA declarations and app-only UserDefaults manifest entry cover these records. The generic reminder sent to Apple contains no baby name or diary records. Delivery is best effort and the service cannot know changes that remain offline on another device.

## External recipients and choices

An invited caregiver can access the family's diary. Apple handles Sign in with Apple and APNs delivery. The production server (Hetzner, Helsinki) and its Litestream backup replica (a Bunny.net storage zone in Germany) host the service data; the repository does not establish a third-party analytics, advertising, email or AI processing integration. The app does not use HealthKit or upload address-book contacts. `CONTACTS` in the declaration describes the in-app caregiver relationship, not a device-address-book permission.

Notification and Live Activity switches are device-scoped. The app offers caregiver removal, leaving a family, family deletion, and account deletion. The iPhone refreshes membership on foreground entry; a removed caregiver’s local offline cache and pending commands are not automatically erased during that refresh. Sign-out clears the local cache only after pending changes are resolved. The ownership-transfer rule above applies to account deletion. There is no self-service account-data export yet; access requests go to the privacy contact in the public policy. The policy should not claim an export feature until one exists. The age-rating declaration marks health/wellness topics and user-generated content because caregivers enter and share diary records inside a family; it does not claim public social media or medical treatment information.

Temperature readings use the existing `HEALTH` and `OTHER_USER_CONTENT` App Store privacy categories and the existing health/wellness age-rating answer. The privacy manifest already declares linked health data for app functionality, so this feature adds no new declaration category or purpose.

The saved Watch action uses those same diary categories and the existing app-only UserDefaults declaration. It does not add an App Store collection category or change the age-rating answer.

## Change checklist

Before adding a user-linked field, new processor, analytics event, retention rule, or new app capability:

1. Record what is collected, why, where it is stored, and whether a caregiver can see another caregiver's entry.
2. Decide how it is removed on item deletion, sign-out, family deletion and account erasure, including backups and pending offline commands.
3. Update `privacy-data-inventory.json`, the App Store declaration, the app privacy manifest, public notices and age-rating answers when affected.
4. Run `mise run privacy:check`, `mise run store -- validate`, and the relevant backend or Apple tests.
5. Plan remote App Store Connect drift before applying or publishing questionnaire changes.

Live Activity reliability uses the existing device/notification identifiers and delivery records. The phone stores latest device tokens, pending activity tokens/session IDs, registration counters and pending timestamps in device-only after-first-unlock Keychain storage. Sign-out removes that record; switching authenticated users removes old activity work. Successful registration removes pending activity work, and Sync prunes work for inaccessible/deleted/superseded sessions. Server revision watermarks remain with their existing device/start rows until device/account or referenced session/family deletion. No new collection category, external recipient or age-rating answer is introduced. Existing DEVICE_ID/HEALTH/OTHER_USAGE_DATA categories and privacy-manifest purposes remain applicable. Client diagnostics include only failure status, queue size and pending age, never raw tokens or child content; log retention remains operator-managed.

Huckleberry CSV import parses the selected file on the phone and retains only completed sleep records and their existing sleep context fields. The CSV itself and other activity types are not uploaded or stored. Imported records and durable pending commands use the existing diary storage, caregiver access, deletion and retention rules. No new stored fields, external processors, collection purposes, privacy declaration categories, or age-rating answers are introduced.
